# Under One Sky — Ethics and risk plan

## Editorial risk

The primary editorial danger is turning complex living traditions into a visual cliché: “ancient monks meet modern telescope.”

The project will resist this by:

- building stories around individuals rather than symbols;
- documenting disagreement and variation;
- avoiding staged symbolic encounters;
- distinguishing doctrine, folklore, metaphor, pedagogy, and personal belief;
- fact-checking both scientific and cultural claims;
- refusing “Buddhism anticipated modern physics” claims unless a source is explicitly being quoted and contextualized.

## Consent

Participants should understand:

- what is being recorded;
- who the project leader is;
- that National Geographic funding is being sought/used;
- that work may reach international audiences;
- that photography, film, audio, text, digital, educational, and immersive adaptations may be created;
- that participation is voluntary.

Consent processes should match context and literacy level rather than relying only on legalistic forms.

## Minors

For minors:

- obtain guardian/institutional permission where appropriate;
- obtain age-appropriate assent;
- avoid sensitive questions unrelated to the story;
- do not expose identifying information that could create risk;
- use school/monastic-school access transparently.

## Monastics

Respect:

- monastery rules;
- gender/access rules;
- ritual boundaries;
- time restrictions;
- local norms around filming sacred spaces or activities.

Monastic status does not replace informed consent.

## Migrants and Myanmar-connected participants

Potential risks include:

- immigration vulnerability;
- political exposure;
- family members remaining in Myanmar;
- fear of authorities;
- unwanted identification.

Mitigations:

- avoid collecting unnecessary sensitive data;
- offer anonymity or reduced identification where editorially possible;
- do not geotag vulnerable participants;
- avoid publishing politically sensitive statements unless necessary, informed, and safe;
- conduct a pre-publication safety review.

## Myanmar travel

Direct travel is optional, not structurally required.

Decision criteria:

- current security conditions;
- legal entry/filming requirements;
- sanctions/compliance implications;
- risk to local collaborators;
- risk to participants after the project team leaves;
- feasibility of lawful permits.

If these conditions are not satisfactory, direct Myanmar fieldwork will be removed.

## Scientific integrity

Astronomy material will be checked against established scientific sources and/or qualified advisers.

The project will clearly separate:

- observable/scientific claims;
- historical claims;
- doctrinal descriptions;
- participant beliefs;
- metaphorical interpretation.

## Cultural integrity

Where possible, cultural advisers will review contextual descriptions for factual errors.

This does not give participants or advisers editorial veto over the story, but it reduces avoidable misrepresentation.

## Data and media management

- maintain redundant encrypted backups;
- separate sensitive contact information from publishable media;
- restrict access to sensitive interview notes;
- document consent status;
- track image/audio/video releases and limitations.

## Physical safety

Budget for:

- insurance;
- safe transport;
- weather protection;
- communications;
- appropriate local guidance;
- health/safety needs for night fieldwork.

## Financial / compliance risk

Before award acceptance, confirm:

- National Geographic payment eligibility;
- sanctions screening;
- banking route;
- tax treatment in Thailand;
- any relevant Thai authorization requirements for funded activity;
- import/export requirements for equipment if applicable.

## Stop rule

No image, interview, or location is worth creating material risk to a participant or collaborator.

If a story can only be obtained by asking a vulnerable person to accept disproportionate risk, that story is removed.
