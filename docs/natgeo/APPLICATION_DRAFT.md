# Project summary

**Under One Sky** is a documentary and immersive storytelling project about what happens when inherited Buddhist cosmologies meet the universe revealed by modern astronomy.

Across Buddhist communities in Thailand and Myanmar-related contexts, I will follow monks, teachers, children, and laypeople as they encounter ideas about the night sky, deep time, planets, galaxies, rebirth, cosmic cycles, and humanity's place in existence.

The project will combine documentary photography, oral histories, field recordings, short-form film, written narrative, and an experimental fulldome adaptation for planetariums.

Its purpose is not to decide whether religious cosmology or scientific astronomy is “correct.” The project asks a human question: **How do people preserve meaning when their picture of the universe changes?**

The final body of work will document continuity, tension, reinterpretation, and wonder across generations while preserving voices that are rarely represented in global discussions of science and religion.

# Core question

**How do people preserve meaning when their picture of the universe changes?**

---

# Why this story matters

Modern astronomy has transformed humanity's physical picture of reality. Yet scientific cosmology does not simply erase older cultural cosmologies. Traditions continue to shape language, ritual, ethics, architecture, education, memory, and identity long after literal understandings change.

Theravada Buddhist communities offer an unusually rich setting in which to document this process. Their inherited cosmologies contain immense temporal scales, multiple realms, cycles of world formation and destruction, and elaborate relationships between human life and a much larger universe.

Today these same communities live with smartphones, astronomy apps, school science, satellite imagery, space missions, and global media.

This project examines the meeting point without reducing it to a contest between “ancient belief” and “modern fact.” It documents how real people negotiate both.

The broader significance extends beyond Buddhism. Every culture must decide what to preserve, reinterpret, metaphorize, or relinquish when knowledge changes. **Under One Sky** uses one specific cultural setting to tell a universal story about continuity and change.

# Objectives

1. **Document living Buddhist cosmologies** through the voices and daily lives of monks, teachers, children, families, and lay practitioners.
2. **Observe encounters with modern astronomy** in educational, religious, and informal contexts.
3. **Record intergenerational differences** in how cosmological narratives are understood, taught, questioned, or reinterpreted.
4. **Preserve oral histories and visual culture** connected to cosmological knowledge.
5. **Create a rigorous, non-exoticizing narrative** that does not force science and religion into a false binary.
6. **Produce multiple storytelling outputs** that can reach photographic, documentary, educational, digital, and planetarium audiences.
7. **Develop an immersive fulldome adaptation** that translates the project into a spatial storytelling form without turning the grant into a technology project.

---

# Story approach

The project will be character-led.

Rather than beginning with abstract doctrine, each chapter will begin with a person, place, or encounter.

Potential narrative scenes include:

- children seeing Saturn's rings through a telescope for the first time;
- a monk describing inherited Buddhist cosmology;
- a science teacher explaining the observable universe;
- younger Buddhists discussing what they interpret literally, symbolically, or ethically;
- night-sky observation beside a monastery;
- smartphones and astronomy software used inside traditional religious environments;
- temple architecture and imagery that encode cosmological ideas;
- family conversations about what older cosmological stories still mean;
- a teacher deciding how to present religious tradition alongside science;
- moments where scientific scale produces awe rather than conflict.

The strongest scenes will emerge from real access and observation, not from staging symbolic juxtapositions for the camera.

# Storytelling media

## Documentary photography

A tightly edited photographic narrative centered on people, place, ritual, learning, and encounters with the night sky.

## Oral histories

Recorded interviews with monks, teachers, students, families, and cultural knowledge holders.

## Short documentary film

A concise character-led film combining observational scenes, interviews, field sound, and astronomy imagery.

## Written narrative

Long-form contextual writing connecting lived stories with Buddhist cosmology, cultural history, and contemporary astronomy.

## Field recordings

Ambient sound, chanting, bells, classrooms, conversations, night environments, and silence.

## Digital story

A web-native sequence integrating photography, audio, text, diagrams, and selected interview excerpts.

## Fulldome adaptation

An immersive version designed for planetariums and educational domes. This is an additional narrative format, not the reason for the project.

# Editorial safeguards

This project will not:

- present monks as visual symbols detached from individual lives;
- stage “ancient versus modern” scenes merely for dramatic effect;
- claim that Buddhist cosmology predicts modern science;
- claim that modern science invalidates the cultural or ethical value of Buddhist traditions;
- homogenize Buddhist communities;
- conceal disagreement or uncertainty;
- pressure vulnerable people to participate;
- publish material that creates avoidable political, legal, or security risk.

---

# Ethics and consent

All identifiable participants will be informed about:

- the purpose of the project;
- the media being recorded;
- the possibility of international publication;
- the possibility of use in photography, film, digital, educational, and immersive formats;
- their right to decline participation.

Special care will be used with:

- minors;
- monastic-school students;
- migrants;
- politically vulnerable participants;
- participants whose statements could create risk in Myanmar-related contexts.

Parental/guardian and institutional permissions will be obtained where appropriate for minors.

Local translators/fixers will be briefed on confidentiality and informed-consent expectations.

# Myanmar-related risk strategy

The project does not depend on unrestricted travel inside Myanmar.

If direct fieldwork there is unsafe, legally constrained, or ethically inappropriate, the project can complete its core objectives through:

- Myanmar Buddhist communities in Thailand;
- migrant and diaspora communities;
- monasteries with Myanmar connections;
- remote interviews where safe;
- archival and historical materials;
- Thailand-based comparative fieldwork.

No participant will be asked to take political risk for the sake of the project.

# Short pitch

For centuries, Theravada Buddhist communities have inherited a cosmos of immense cycles, multiple realms, and richly symbolic worlds. Today they live with galaxies, exoplanets, black holes, smartphones, and modern science education. **Under One Sky** follows monks, teachers, children, and families in Thailand and Myanmar-connected communities to document what survives, what changes, and what still gives meaning when humanity's picture of the universe is transformed.
