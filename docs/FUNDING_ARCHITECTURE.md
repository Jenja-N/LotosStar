# Lotos Star — Funding Architecture

## Principle

Lotos Star should not be reshaped every time a new funder appears.

Instead, divide the project into layers and match each funding source to the layer it can support without controlling the whole system.

> **The project remains coherent. Funding remains modular.**

---

## Layer 1 — Founder / Prototype

### Object
Prototype 01.

### Needs
- founder time;
- engineering;
- dome;
- projection/audio;
- software;
- prototype content;
- pilots;
- documentation.

### Best funding forms
- fellowships;
- unrestricted creator/builder grants;
- R&D grants;
- prototype sponsorship;
- mission-aligned philanthropy.

### Example fit
O'Shaughnessy Fellowship.

### Guardrail
Do not give away strategic control merely to finance the first prototype.

## Founder ownership and control

The desired structure is:

- founder retains core strategic direction;
- conceptual IP remains controlled by the founder;
- software can remain open-source where useful;
- commercial rights can be licensed selectively;
- grant-funded works follow their specific grant terms;
- partners receive rights only to the layer they fund or operate.

Avoid broad agreements that unintentionally transfer:

- trademarks;
- future hub rights;
- derivative concept rights;
- global exclusivity;
- founder IP;
- unrelated future content.

## Money hierarchy

Spend first on things that create evidence.

### Tier 1 — Essential
- founder time;
- physical proof;
- projection;
- audio;
- original content;
- pilots;
- documentation.

### Tier 2 — Valuable
- improved fabrication;
- expanded program library;
- research collaboration;
- translation;
- accessibility;
- professional film documentation.

### Tier 3 — Later
- permanent luxury architecture;
- hospitality complex;
- large residential component;
- extensive wellness infrastructure;
- speculative global expansion;
- expensive branding.

## Russian citizenship / Thailand base

Founder citizenship: Russian Federation.

Current base: Thailand.

Funding strategy must therefore include a practical compliance layer.

For every grant/fellowship:

1. verify applicant nationality restrictions;
2. verify residency restrictions;
3. verify sanctions/compliance policy;
4. verify payment route;
5. verify bank acceptance;
6. verify tax consequences in Thailand and any other relevant jurisdiction;
7. verify whether local activity requires additional legal/work authorization.

Do this before investing heavily in an application.

Do not assume:
- Russian citizenship automatically disqualifies;
- Thailand residency automatically resolves all compliance issues;
- a U.S./EU grant can necessarily pay any bank/account;
- a “grant” is automatically tax-free.

---

## Funding decision test

Before accepting money, ask:

1. What exactly is being funded?
2. What deliverable is promised?
3. Who owns the resulting IP?
4. What publication/license rights are granted?
5. Is exclusivity involved?
6. Does the funder gain influence over unrelated layers?
7. Can the project continue if this funder disappears?
8. Does the agreement make global replication harder?
9. Does it preserve founder autonomy?
10. Does the money produce proof or merely overhead?

---

## Strategic target

The ideal funding architecture makes Lotos Star **harder to capture**.

No single sponsor should be able to say:

> “Because we funded one program, we now control the institution.”

The desired model is:

> **one project, many compatible funding channels, clear rights boundaries, retained founder direction.**
