# Lotos Star — OSV Fellowship Strategy

## Core positioning

The O'Shaughnessy Fellowship application should not present Lotos Star as a request to finance a global network.

It should present **Evgeny Kuznetsov as the builder** and **Prototype 01 as the one-year proof**.

The central question:

> **What if the planetarium replaced the classroom?**

The central promise:

> Give me one focused year and enough resources to build the first working proof of a new cultural institution.

---

## One-sentence version

**I am building a transformable planetarium that functions simultaneously as a school, laboratory, theatre, and contemplative space — then publishing the technical and educational model so it can be reproduced elsewhere.**

---

## Why this is a strong fellowship-shaped project

Lotos Star is difficult to place inside a conventional institution.

It is simultaneously:

- education;
- engineering;
- immersive media;
- science communication;
- cultural storytelling;
- art;
- contemplative practice;
- open technical development.

That ambiguity is a weakness in conventional hiring and a potential strength in a fellowship designed to back unusual independent builders.

The application should therefore avoid trying to look conventional.

---

## Founder–project fit

The strongest argument is not a list of credentials. It is the fact that the project requires an unusual combination of lived experience and execution skills.

Relevant founder evidence:

- 20+ years in technology, product/project work, operations, and team leadership;
- university teaching;
- programming and engineering background;
- education work in Myanmar;
- Buddhist monastic and contemplative experience;
- photography across dozens of countries;
- published books and long-form writing;
- ability to lead complex interdisciplinary work.

The application should frame these not as separate careers but as **inputs into one project**.

---

## What not to say

Avoid:

- “global decentralized ecosystem” as the opening;
- generic claims about innovation;
- long lists of technologies;
- inflated language about changing humanity;
- real-estate language;
- vague promises of “community” and “wellness”;
- claims that the global network already exists;
- presenting every possible future module at once.

Do not lead with:
VR, AR, AI, sustainability, blockchain, digital nomads, wellness, tourism, coworking, global network.

Those may exist later, but they weaken the first proof.

---

## What to say instead

Lead with:

1. one physical object — the dome;
2. one institutional thesis — integrated learning;
3. one year — Prototype 01;
4. one measurable outcome — real people using it;
5. one public artifact — documented reproducible model.

## Suggested application narrative

### Opening

> What if the planetarium replaced the classroom?

Most institutions separate science, art, culture, and attention. I am building one physical environment where they can meet again.

### Origin

The project emerged from years spent moving between engineering, education, photography, writing, and Buddhist communities in Myanmar and Thailand.

The key insight was not technological. It was institutional:

> people experience reality as one world, but our institutions teach it as disconnected subjects.

### Project

Lotos Star uses a transformable planetarium as both infrastructure and medium.

The same dome can host:

- astronomy;
- scientific visualization;
- documentary;
- cultural storytelling;
- generative art;
- music;
- lectures;
- contemplative practice.

### Fellowship ask

The request is not to fund the entire future network.

The request is:

> fund one year in which I can build, test, document, and publicly prove Prototype 01.

### Outcome

At the end of the year, success is not a pitch deck.

Success is a place someone can enter.

## Evidence before application

The repository should increasingly show that the project is already moving without funding.

Priority evidence:

### Technical
- dome CAD;
- projection tests;
- BOM;
- architecture diagrams;
- software prototype.

### Content
- one short fulldome demo;
- one KosmoLingua lesson/program;
- one cultural story prototype.

### Field
- photographs;
- interviews;
- site experiments;
- partner conversations.

### Execution
- dated build log;
- decisions and revisions;
- issue tracker;
- milestone history.

A strong fellowship application should make the reviewer think:

> **This person is already doing it. Funding changes the speed and scale, not the existence of the project.**

## Funding logic

If awarded flexible fellowship funding, the money should buy:

- founder time;
- prototype hardware;
- fabrication;
- projection/audio equipment;
- content production;
- scientific/cultural consulting;
- fieldwork;
- documentation;
- pilot operations.

The fellowship should not be framed as a salary for doing nothing else.

It should be framed as:

> **removing the economic friction that prevents full-time construction of the proof.**

---

## Intellectual property

Lotos Star remains the founder's project.

The repository can continue to separate:

- conceptual IP;
- open-source software;
- grant-funded content;
- commercial licensing where relevant.

Any fellowship application should use the current OSV terms in force at the time of submission and should not rely on older archived descriptions.

## Citizenship and location

Founder: Russian citizen, based in Thailand.

The application should state this plainly where requested and avoid unnecessary political framing.

Before accepting any award, confirm:

- payment eligibility;
- tax treatment;
- sanctions/compliance requirements;
- banking route available to the fellowship;
