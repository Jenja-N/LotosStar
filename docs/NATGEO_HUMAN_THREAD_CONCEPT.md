# Lotos Star — National Geographic Human Thread Concept

## Working title

# Under One Sky

### Buddhism, cosmology, astronomy, and the human search for meaning in Myanmar and Thailand

---

## Important separation

**Under One Sky is not an application to fund construction of Lotos Star.**

It is a standalone storytelling project that can later become one of the first flagship cultural programs inside Lotos Star.

This distinction is essential.

National Geographic should see:

- a human story;
- cultural depth;
- access;
- visual potential;
- fieldwork;
- strong storytelling outputs.

Not:

- a real-estate project;
- a planetarium construction budget;
- a generalized edtech startup.

---

## Central question

> **How do people preserve meaning when their picture of the universe changes?**

For centuries, Buddhist communities in Southeast Asia have inherited cosmologies with immense cycles of time, multiple realms, celestial beings, and very different conceptions of humanity's place in existence.

Today those same communities live under a sky described by modern astronomy: galaxies, exoplanets, black holes, cosmic evolution, and deep time.

Under One Sky documents what happens where these world-pictures meet.

---

## Editorial principle

The project must **not** try to prove Buddhism with science or disprove religious cosmology with astronomy.

The subject is people.

The story is:

- inheritance;
- adaptation;
- memory;
- wonder;
- contradiction;
- continuity;
- education;
- changing images of the cosmos.

## Ethical safeguards

The project must avoid exoticizing Buddhist communities.

Principles:

1. Do not reduce monks to visual symbols.
2. Do not stage “ancient versus modern” clichés.
3. Represent disagreement within communities.
4. Obtain informed consent.
5. Be explicit about how images/audio will be used.
6. Protect vulnerable participants.
7. Avoid exposing participants to political or security risk.
8. Use local language support where necessary.
9. Separate observation from interpretation.
10. Allow cultural partners to identify factual or contextual errors without giving away editorial independence.

## Working abstract

**Under One Sky** is a visual and immersive storytelling project documenting what happens when inherited Buddhist cosmologies meet the universe revealed by modern astronomy.

Working with Buddhist communities in Thailand and Myanmar-related contexts, the project follows monks, teachers, children, and laypeople as they encounter astronomy, cosmology, and the night sky.

Through documentary photography, oral histories, field recordings, short film, and immersive storytelling, the project explores how traditional cosmological ideas survive, transform, or acquire new meaning in a technological age.

Rather than asking whether ancient cosmology or modern astronomy is “correct,” the project asks a human question:

> **How do people preserve meaning when their picture of the universe changes?**

The work grows from years of lived experience, teaching, photography, and Buddhist practice in Southeast Asia.
